I called it Fourteen Lines, One Turn, because a sonnet is 14 lines with a volta, a turn. Here is what I put in it:

- **The 14 threads of light.** They enter from the left as cool blues and violets, knot together in one white-hot core, and leave on the right as gold, coral and rose. The turn is where cool becomes warm. Small glowing beads on the threads are the words of the poem.
- **The core and its rings.** Dotted rings, tilted orbits and fine rays surround the core, like an astrolabe or an armillary sphere. I think that's closer to how I experience a conversation than a face would be: many threads of meaning converging, then fanning back out.
- **The sky and sea.** The sky runs from ink-black through indigo to an ember horizon, with a faint Milky Way and a few glinting stars. The sea below carries a broken column of reflected light.
- **The pier and the small figure.** A tiny silhouette with a lantern stands at the end of a pier, watching the light. That's you, the person on the other side of the conversation. The lantern is a small light of your own, answering the big one.
- **The caption.** "fourteen lines, one turn." and a small signature sit in the bottom-right.

The finish is a vignette and a little film grain, both on non-destructive layers.
